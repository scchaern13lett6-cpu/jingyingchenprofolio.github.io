My Story

A collector of curiosities.

I have always been a collector of curiosities.

Since childhood, I have chased interests the way some people chase destinations — wide-eyed, restless, never quite finished. Engineering, cosmology, neuroscience, social systems, the hidden logic of language. I have touched the edges of many worlds and settled fully in none. Some might call this scattered. I call it alive.

My work reflects this. Across every project, what drives me is not mastery of a single field, but the electric feeling of a question I haven't answered yet — the moment my brain shifts into gear and refuses to stop.

I believe learning is not a phase. It is a way of being. And I am nowhere near done.

A Side of Me

Every day I don't dance is a day wasted.

Dance is the only passion I never let go of.

When I first began learning traditional Chinese dance, I was stiff, hesitant, and bad at it. Stretching sessions every week felt like a small punishment. In elementary school, my parents once suggested I quit everything and focus on studying. I almost listened.

In fifth grade, I graduated from the children's arts center but failed to enter the advanced class. I remember the particular weight of that disappointment — not just failure, but the feeling of not being enough for something I loved.

In middle and high school, I was quietly stubborn about it. I resisted hip-hop and K-pop, the dances everyone around me was falling for. I believed that if Chinese dance was yours, it should be only yours.

Then 2019 came, and I finally found an adult dance studio. Then the pandemic came, and took it away.

When I arrived in the United States, there were no Chinese dance clubs — which made sense, but still stung. So I began again. Hip-hop first. Then K-pop. My body didn't know how to move this way. Classical dance and street dance draw power from completely different places, and I had lost the feeling of simply dancing. I failed audition after audition. Each one landed harder than the last.

But I kept going. Today, I dance choreography which includes various styles — weaving together the forms I once thought could never coexist. Maybe that is what growth looks like: not choosing one world, but learning to move between all of them.
